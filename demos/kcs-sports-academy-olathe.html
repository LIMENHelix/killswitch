The credentials

Anyone can call themselves a speed coach.

Youth performance training is an unlicensed field, which makes the letters the only real standard in it. Ted holds four, from two national bodies, and every one of them can be checked. Here is what each is worth to your athlete.

National Sports
Performance Association
C.S.A.C.
Certified Speed & Agility Coach
Sprint mechanics · acceleration · change of direction

The credential that separates a speed coach from a conditioning coach. NSPA's course is built around improving speed and agility for an athlete in any sport: how the foot strikes, how an athlete accelerates out of a cut, how to change direction without giving up a step. The goal is not more running. It is better running.

National Academy
of Sports Medicine
C.P.T.
Certified Personal Trainer
The accredited foundation · programming · safe progression

NASM's CPT is the NCCA-accredited base credential and the most widely recognised certification in fitness. It is the one that says a coach was examined on anatomy, program design and how to load a body safely, before ever handing a young athlete a workout.

National Academy
of Sports Medicine
C.E.S.
Corrective Exercise Specialist
Find the movement fault before it becomes an injury

This is the parent's credential. CES is movement assessment and correction: spotting the knee that collapses inward, the ankle that will not bend, the hip that never fully extends, then fixing the cause instead of stretching the symptom. Growing athletes compensate faster than they adapt, and a compensation trained at speed is how a season ends early.

National Academy
of Sports Medicine
P.E.S.
Performance Enhancement Specialist
Strength and conditioning built for a sport, not a gym

NASM's strength-and-conditioning track: plyometrics, speed, agility, resistance training and Olympic lifting, organised across a season rather than dropped in at random. It is what turns a number in the weight room into a faster first step in a real game.

Verify any of them yourself. That is the point of a certification.

The coach

Ted Johnson

Movement Specialist · Owner

Ted runs KC's Sports Academy Youth Sports Performance in Olathe, coaching speed, movement quality and sport conditioning for young athletes across the Kansas City metro.

He is a certified speed coach through the National Sports Performance Association and holds three credentials from the National Academy of Sports Medicine: Certified Personal Trainer, Corrective Exercise Specialist and Performance Enhancement Specialist. That combination is deliberate. One covers how to make an athlete faster, one covers how to load a body safely, one covers how to find the movement fault first, and one covers how to build a season around all three.

In his own words, the academy develops young athletes "using adaptation and progression to promote efficiency and maximum effectiveness on the field or court."

Boys and girls, field sports and court sports. Team enquiries go to teams@kcssportsacademy.com.
