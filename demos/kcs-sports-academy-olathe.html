The physics

The mark is Newton's second law, wearing a pyramid.

1
F·Δt=m·Δv
Impulse–momentum theorem. Force applied over time changes momentum. The whole of sprint acceleration, in five symbols.
÷ m divide both sides by the athlete's mass
2
Δv=Fm·Δt
A heavier athlete needs more force for the same change in speed. This is why force gets coached relative to bodyweight, not in absolute pounds.
F/m = a and force over mass is acceleration
3
V=A×T
The academy mark. Velocity, acceleration, time. Which is why the two at the base carry the one at the top: they are what produce it.
v(t) = Vmax (1 − e−t/τ)

Velocity does not rise in a straight line, it follows this curve. τ sets how hard an athlete accelerates, Vmax sets where they top out, and two split times solve for both. That is what the first tool below does.

The same models a sports scientist would use, running in your browser. Bring a stopwatch and a measured distance. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent, and you do not have to book anything to use them.

Sprint Profile

Samozino et al. 20161

Two split times and a bodyweight give you top speed, how hard the athlete accelerates, and the force they put into the ground.

How to run it: one all-out sprint from a standing start. Take a split at a short distance and again at a long one, e.g. 10 and 40 yards. Both splits must come from the same run.

Acceleration Check

Uniform acceleration · d = ½at²

One split from a dead stop, turned into average acceleration and the speed reached at that line.

How to run it: start from a complete standstill, no rolling start, and time to a single marked line. Short distances work best, ten to twenty yards.

Flying Speed

Max velocity · v = d / t

True top speed, measured in a zone the athlete enters already at full pace, with the acceleration taken out.

How to run it: give a run-up of at least 20 yards, then time a short flying zone, e.g. 10 yards. The clock starts as they enter the zone, not when they start running.

Stride Mechanics

v = stride length × frequency

Splits speed into its two parts, so you can see whether an athlete is short-striding or slow-turning-over.

How to run it: film the run on a phone and count every foot contact across the timed distance. Both feet count. Left, right, left is three.

Models as published — Samozino et al., sprint force–velocity profiling, Morin sprint FVP spreadsheet. Hand timing carries roughly a tenth of a second of error, so treat these as a baseline to re-test against, not a verdict.

The method

Velocity. Acceleration. Time.

Acceleration and top speed are not the same quality, they are not produced the same way, and they are not trained the same way. In elite athletes the biggest differences over short distances show up at two and five meters, and they track how well force is put into the ground horizontally.2

Apex

Velocity

Top speed. Reached past roughly thirty meters, running tall, where force turns vertical and the foot is on the ground for a fraction of a second. It is also the first thing fatigue takes: after a match, top-end velocity degrades more than acceleration does.7

How it is trainedUpright mechanics and flying runs, entered at speed rather than from a start. Big vertical force in very short ground contacts, a phase that responds differently to load than acceleration does.5

Acceleration

How quickly speed is gained. The first steps out of a stance, a cut or a stop. Force goes into the ground behind the athlete, at roughly forty-five degrees.

How it is trainedShort repeated efforts inside the first ten to thirty meters, plus resisted work. Eight weeks of resisted sled training has been shown to improve 5 m and 20 m sprint times.3 The coaching point is the direction of force, not the size of it.

The credentials

Anyone can call themselves a speed coach.

Youth performance training is an unlicensed field, which makes the letters the only real standard in it. Ted holds four, from two national bodies, and every one of them can be checked. Here is what each is worth to your athlete.

National Sports
Performance Association

Certified Speed & Agility Coach
Sprint mechanics · acceleration · change of direction

The credential that separates a speed coach from a conditioning coach. NSPA's course is built around improving speed and agility for an athlete in any sport: how the foot strikes, how an athlete accelerates out of a cut, how to change direction without giving up a step. The goal is not more running. It is better running.

Certified Personal Trainer
The accredited foundation · programming · safe progression

NASM's CPT is the NCCA-accredited base credential and the most widely recognized certification in fitness. It is the one that says a coach was examined on anatomy, program design and how to load a body safely, before ever handing a young athlete a workout.

National Academy
of Sports Medicine
C.E.S.
Corrective Exercise Specialist
Find the movement fault before it becomes an injury

This is the parent's credential. CES is movement assessment and correction: spotting the knee that collapses inward, the ankle that will not bend, the hip that never fully extends, then fixing the cause instead of stretching the symptom. Growing athletes compensate faster than they adapt, and a compensation trained at speed is how a season ends early.

National Academy

Strength and conditioning built for a sport, not a gym

NASM's strength-and-conditioning track: plyometrics, speed, agility, resistance training and Olympic lifting, organized across a season rather than dropped in at random. It is what turns a number in the weight room into a faster first step in a real game.

Inside a block

Every one of those letters is a method, not a badge.

Each credential is built on a published framework that a coach is examined on before they are awarded it. Below is what NSPA and NASM actually require behind each set of initials, in their own sequence. It is why a certified coach and an enthusiastic one are not the same purchase.

Stage 01

Screen the movement

NASM C.E.S.

NASM's Corrective Exercise Continuum is a fixed four-step order for anything the screen turns up.

  1. Inhibit quiet the overactive tissue
  2. Lengthen restore the range that was lost
  3. Activate wake up what stopped working
  4. Integrate put it back into a real pattern

Stretching the sore part treats the symptom. This sequence goes after the cause, in that order, every time.

Stage 02

Build the engine

NASM C.P.T. · P.E.S.

NASM's OPT model is a periodized progression through three levels, and an athlete earns the next one.

  1. Stabilization can the pattern be held under control
  2. Strength add speed of execution and change of direction
  3. Power game speed, and drills that stop being predictable

Jump training follows the same ladder. Early on a landing is held for three to five seconds before the next rep. Later it becomes repetitive and reactive, because by then the landing is safe.

Stage 03

Take it to the field

NSPA C.S.A.C.

NSPA's speed course is built around four training targets, coached separately because they are different skills.

  1. Acceleration horizontal force, the first ten to thirty meters
  2. Max velocity vertical force, tall posture, short contacts
  3. Agility cutting and changing direction without losing a step
  4. Game speed the same qualities when the drill stops being predictable

Speed that only shows up in a straight line on a turf lane was never the point. It has to survive a game.

Methodology as published by the certifying bodies — NASM Corrective Exercise Continuum, NASM OPT Model, NSPA Certified Speed & Agility Coach.
