## 1. The reposition

| | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| **What we sell** | Websites in minutes | **Your entire online presence, handled.** |
| **The website** | The product | The free foot in the door |
| **Who does it** | Tool / AI / speed | A real person you can text |
| **How people find us** | Followers, audience, content | A person messaging them: local FB groups, DMs to businesses with bad or no sites |
| **What we charge for** | The build | Keeping everything handled, month after month |

**One line:** *Sunflower handles your whole online presence — website, Google, reviews,
socials, the stuff you never get to. The website's free. You deal with a person, not a bot.*

**Why "handled" and not "in minutes":** "In minutes" sells speed, and speed is
something every AI builder now gives away. A busy owner doesn't want a faster
way to do the work. They want the work to stop being theirs. "Handled" is the job
they'd hire someone for. Speed becomes a footnote ("and yes, it's fast").

**Why anti-AI is the angle:** owners are drowning in automated pitches, AI
builders, and chatbot support. The rarest thing on offer is *a named human who
answers*. That's the moat. Lean on it everywhere: a name, a face, a phone number,
"text me" over "submit a form."

---

## 2. What "your entire online presence" means

Spell it out. Vague "presence" reads as fluff; a checklist reads as relief.

- **Website** — built for you, free. Yours to keep.
- **Google Business Profile** — set up, verified, hours/photos/services kept current.
- **Reviews** — we ask your happy customers for them and reply to every one.
- **Facebook / Instagram** — page cleaned up, kept alive, no ghost-town feeds.
- **Listings** — Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, Nextdoor: same name, address, phone everywhere.
- **Changes** — new prices, new hours, holiday closure: text us, it's done.

The free site is item one. Everything after it is the monthly relationship.

---

## 3. Cut list (remove from the site and bios)

- [ ] Any "in minutes" / "instant" / "launch in X minutes" headline. Speed can appear once, low on the page, as a reassurance.
- [ ] "AI-powered", "AI builder", "generated", "automated" as selling points. If AI is used behind the scenes, it's not the pitch.
- [ ] Follower counts, "join our community", "follow us for tips", newsletter-as-primary-CTA. Sunflower isn't an audience play.
- [ ] Chatbot widgets. Replace with "Text [Name]" + a real number.
- [ ] DIY language: "build your own", "drag and drop", "templates", "editor".
- [ ] Generic "contact us" forms as the only path. A form can stay; it can't be the headline CTA.

## 5. The sales motion

Sunflower grows one conversation at a time. Two channels, same voice.

### A. Local Facebook groups

Rules: read the group's rules first; post only where business promos are allowed
(or on the designated promo day). Never spam the same text across 10 groups the
same hour. Help in comments more than you post.

**Intro post**
> Hey [Group] 👋 I'm [Name], I live in [Town] and build websites for local
> businesses. Free. No catch hiding in the fine print: I make my money if you
> later want me to keep your Google, reviews and socials handled. Most don't
> need that on day one.
> If your site is outdated, broken on phones, or you don't have one, comment or
> message me your business name and I'll send a free preview. Real person, I
> answer my own messages.

**Comment reply** (when someone asks "anyone know a web person?")
> I do this locally, [Name] here. Happy to put together a free preview so you
> can see it before deciding anything. Messaging you now.

### B. DMs to businesses with bad or no websites

Source: Google/FB searches for local businesses with no site, a dead site, or a
site that breaks on a phone. One specific detail per message, or don't send it.

**No website**
> Hi [Owner], [Name] here, I'm local in [Town]. Saw [Business] on Facebook,
> great reviews, but no website comes up when I Google you. I'd like to build
> you one, free, yours to keep. Want me to send a preview?

**Bad / outdated website**
> Hi [Owner], [Name] here from [Town]. Pulled up [Business]'s site on my phone
> and [specific: the menu's cut off / it still says 2019 hours / it won't load].
> I rebuild sites for local businesses, free. Want me to mock up a new one so
> you can see it?

**Follow-up (once, 3–4 days later)**
> No pressure at all, just didn't want this to get buried. Preview's still on
> the table if you want it.

Then stop. One follow-up, never three.

### C. From "yes" to monthly

1. Send the free preview (fast — this is where speed actually helps).
2. Launch it. Hand over ownership.
3. ~2 weeks later, check in as a person: "How's it going? Getting calls from it?"
4. Offer the handled plan only once they've seen you deliver:
   > "Want me to keep Google, reviews and your FB page handled too? Flat [$X]/mo,
   > cancel any time, you just text me changes."

## 6. Voice rules

- First person singular ("I"), with a name. Not "our team", not "we leverage".
- Specific over clever: their town, their trade, the actual broken thing.
- Name the catch before they ask.
- Every CTA is a human action: text, message, reply. Never "get started" alone.
- If a sentence could come from any AI website builder, rewrite it.
